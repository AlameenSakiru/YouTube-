## Description

```
On Sunday, November 9, 1913, the steel freighter Henry B. Smith sailed out of Marquette, Michigan, into the deadliest storm in Great Lakes history, with 25 men on board. Twenty minutes later the storm hit her with its full force, and she vanished. For a hundred years the only clues were wreckage on the beaches east of Marquette and a note in a bottle that said she broke in two, 12 miles east of the town. In May 2013, three shipwreck hunters found her 535 feet down, about 30 miles north. Broken in two, just as the note said, but nowhere near where it said. We follow the clues, the search and what the wreck shows, and ask what really sank her: the hatches, the storm, or the decision to sail.

The pictures in this video are AI illustrations, not photographs or footage of the wreck. The sonar screens, maps and diagrams are illustrations, approximate and not to scale. The wreck is a grave; nothing in this video shows human remains.
```
