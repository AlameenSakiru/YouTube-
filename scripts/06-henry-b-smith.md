Inferences, said as inferences in the script: "turning to port, turning north" (she was heading out from Marquette; the wreck lies north); the wind "would have driven" wreckage toward the beaches east of Marquette; the three suspects.

Care: no detail about the two men who were found. The wreck is called a grave. Image prompts forbid people at the wreck.

## Recheck, 28 November

- "And nobody saw it coming" was wrong: storm warning flags were flying on the 7th and 8th. Now: "Forecasters could see a storm coming. What they couldn't see was how bad it would get."
- "The only clues" became "the best clues": two of the crew were also found, both far to the east (now said in section 5, without detail).
- A claim that a part in the first camera "blew" could not be confirmed and is gone. Replaced with the confirmed June return: a 500-ft tether, caught on the wreck for half an hour, still filmed her name.
- "Their son, Jarrod" became "Jerry's son, Jarrod" (the sources only say Eliason's son); "they've never fully said" became "they kept to themselves" (true in 2013, no claim about since).
- "Almost exactly a hundred years" stays: lost 9–10 November 1913, found 24 May 2013, 99½ years.
- Title: a 59-second Short titled "This Ship Vanished in 1913. It Was Found 100 Years Later" (The American Logbook, 1,272 views) went up on 22 November. A Short sits on a different shelf and our video is 13 minutes, so the main title stays; the backup title is the bottle note.
