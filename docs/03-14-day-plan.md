# The 14-day plan

**Goal:** by day 14, the channel has the views that qualify it for monetization: 4,000 watch hours and 1,000 subscribers. Approval comes later, after YouTube's review.

**What that takes:** about 48,000 views at a 5-minute average watch time (see [01-rules-and-targets.md](01-rules-and-targets.md)). A new channel gets there only if at least one video breaks out and YouTube starts recommending it. No plan can guarantee that. This one is built to give you as many good chances as possible in 14 days, and to react fast when one video starts to move.

Plan for 3–5 hours of work a day.

## How the plan raises your chances

1. **Publish early.** Video 1 goes out on day 3, so it has 11 days to collect views.
2. **Start with your strongest story.** One specific place, a date or number, and a fact that sounds impossible but is documented. See "How to choose video 1" in [04-video-briefs.md](04-video-briefs.md).
3. **Make longer videos that hold attention.** A 15-minute video watched for 7 minutes needs about 34,000 views to reach 4,000 hours. A 10-minute video watched for 4 minutes needs 60,000.
4. **Four videos, then follow the winner.** Days 13–14 are kept free for a fast follow-up to whichever video is doing best.
5. **Fix packaging quickly.** If a video has a low click rate after 48 hours, change the thumbnail or title the same day.

## Days 1–3: video 1

| Day | Tasks |
|---|---|
| **1** | Set up the channel (checklist in [02-niche-and-channel.md](02-niche-and-channel.md)). Choose the story for video 1. Research it from at least three reliable sources and collect pictures you are allowed to use. Decide the title and thumbnail idea. |
| **2** | Write the script (2,000–2,300 words for about 15 minutes). Record the voiceover. |
| **3** | Edit the video. Make three thumbnail versions and pick the clearest. **Publish video 1.** Reply to every comment. |

## Days 4–6: video 2

| Day | Tasks |
|---|---|
| **4** | Research and write the script for video 2. |
| **5** | Record the voiceover and edit video 2. Make the thumbnail and title. |
| **6** | **Publish video 2.** Check video 1's numbers (see "What to check" below). |

## Days 7–9: video 3

| Day | Tasks |
|---|---|
| **7** | Choose the topic for video 3 to be close to whichever of videos 1 and 2 is doing better. Write the script. |
| **8** | Record the voiceover and edit video 3. Make the thumbnail and title. |
| **9** | **Publish video 3.** Change the thumbnail or title of any video with a low click rate. |

## Days 10–12: video 4

| Day | Tasks |
|---|---|
| **10** | Write the script for video 4, again close to your best performer. |
| **11** | Record the voiceover and edit video 4. Make the thumbnail and title. |
| **12** | **Publish video 4.** |

## Days 13–14: follow the winner

| Day | Tasks |
|---|---|
| **13** | Start a follow-up to your best video: a nearby place, the same kind of story, the same title style. Write and record it. |
| **14** | Publish the follow-up if it is ready. Review all your videos and plan the next ten topics. |

## If a video starts to take off

Signs of a breakout, usually on day 2 or 3 after publishing:

- Views per hour are rising, not falling.
- In Analytics → Reach, "Browse features" and "Suggested videos" become the main traffic sources.

When you see this, drop the schedule and do three things:

1. Start a follow-up on the closest related story and publish it within two to three days.
2. Add an end screen and a pinned comment on the breakout video pointing to your other videos.
3. Do not change the title or thumbnail of the video that is working.

## Every day

- Log subscribers and watch hours in the tracker.
- Reply to comments within the first hours after publishing.
- Watch one top video in the niche and write down its first 30 seconds word for word.

## Optional: Shorts for subscribers

Subscribers are often the slower of the two numbers. From each long video, cut one or two clips of 30–50 seconds with the most surprising moment, and link the full video as the related video. Shorts can bring subscribers, but as far as I know their watch time in the Shorts feed does not count toward the 4,000 hours, so never let them take time from the long videos.

## What to check in YouTube Studio

Look at each video 48 hours after publishing.

| Number | Where | Aim for | If it is low |
|---|---|---|---|
| Impressions click-through rate | Analytics → Reach | 4–6% or more | Change the thumbnail or title |
| Average view duration | Analytics → Engagement | 40% of the video length or more | Tighten the script; cut slow parts |
| Viewers still watching at 30 seconds | Retention graph | 65% or more | Rewrite the opening |

These are rough benchmarks, not rules from YouTube. Compare your videos with each other more than with the benchmark.

## If day 14 arrives and the numbers are not there

That is the most common result for a new channel, and it does not mean the plan failed. You will have four or five videos and real data. Keep publishing every three days and keep following your best performer. The date that matters is 1 February 2027, when the watch-hour bar is reported to double, so aim to qualify and apply by early January.
