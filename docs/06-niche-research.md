# Niche research

Done on 11 November 2026. This replaces my two earlier niche picks, which were based on general knowledge and one web search each. This one is based on measurements.

## What I measured

- **Pay:** a published study of real YouTube Studio earnings from 300 channels (AIR Media-Tech, May 2025 to May 2026).
- **Competition and demand:** I read 118 public YouTube topic pages and then the channel pages of every channel found there. That gave **582 channels across 105 topics**. For each channel I recorded subscribers, number of videos, and the views, age and length of its latest 30 videos.

The full channel list is in [research/channels-2026-11-11.csv](../research/channels-2026-11-11.csv). Every channel has a link, so you can check any of it yourself.

## Finding 1: what pays

Median earnings per 1,000 views (RPM), from the 300-channel study:

| Category | Median RPM | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Education & Science | $10.22 | 77% of views carry an ad, the highest in the study |
| Transport | $5.69 | Only 7 channels in the sample |
| Lifestyle | $2.98 | |
| News & Politics | $2.60 | Only 3 channels |
| Entertainment | $2.43 | |
| Gadgets & Tech | $2.33 | |
| Gaming | $2.05 | |
| Business & Finance | $2.01 | Only 3 channels; rises about 60% in October to December |
| Health & Sport | $1.23 | |
| Kids & Teens | $0.33 | |

Three things follow from this:

1. **Long educational documentaries pay best.** Viewers watch for a long time, so more ads are shown per view.
2. **Finance is not the sure winner that most "best niche" articles claim.** Those articles quote what advertisers bid, not what channels receive. This is why my first two picks were weak.
3. **The channel matters more than the category.** Inside Education & Science, the top quarter earns $19.50 and the bottom quarter $2.31.

Where the viewers live also matters: audiences in the US, Canada, the UK and Australia earn the most.

## Finding 4: subscribers are the harder half

Several of these channels passed 4,000 watch hours quickly but are still far below 1,000 subscribers. Right of Way Files has roughly 210,000 views and 452 subscribers.

Rough subscribers gained per 100 views, from the channels I measured:

| Type of channel | Subscribers per 100 views |
|---|---|
| Places and geography | about 1 to 1.5 |
| Railroads, roads, old machines | about 0.2 to 0.9 |

For the same views, a places-and-geography channel reaches 1,000 subscribers two to five times sooner. These are my estimates from visible view counts, not exact figures.

## Finding 5: the niche sets the odds, the title decides

Inside the same niche, on the same day, one channel gets 100,000 views and another gets 50. The channels that copy a successful channel's topics with shorter videos and vaguer titles are the ones getting 50.

I tested 1,467 titles from 66 small channels. A breakout is a video with at least three times its own channel's usual views; about 20% of all videos were breakouts.

| Title feature | Titles | Breakout rate |
|---|---|---|
| "Hiding" or "nobody talks about" | 35 | 43% |
| "Still" | 68 | 29% |
| Danger words (dangerous, deadliest, killed) | 150 | 28% |
| Most, biggest, last, first, only | 182 | 26% |
| Never, nobody, no one | 110 | 26% |
| Starts with Why or How | 180 | 22% |
| Has a year in it | 204 | 16% |
| Hype words (terrifying, disturbing, shocking) | 52 | 15% |
| Two sentences | 89 | 11% |

Titles of 50 to 90 characters did slightly better than shorter or longer ones.

This corrects my earlier advice. I had said a date or number helps. Across these channels a year in the title did not help, and two-sentence titles did worse than average. One road-history channel does very well with exactly that style, but it is the exception.

The biggest breakouts in the Great Lakes region all start with the place name: "Michigan's Upper Peninsula Is More Dangerous Than You Think" (322,000 views), "Wisconsin Is Hiding Something More Dangerous Than You Think" (283,000), "Lake Superior Is WAY More Dangerous Than People Think" (243,000), all from one channel with under 8,000 subscribers.

## Finding 6: where it is crowded and where it is not

New channels (under three months old, 30 videos or fewer) that I found in each lane. "Has a hit" means at least one video over 30,000 views. "Struggling" means no hit and a typical video under 1,000 views.

| Lane | New channels found | Has a hit | Struggling |
|---|---|---|---|
| US roads, bridges, tunnels | 11 | 2 | 8 |
| US railroads | 6 | 2 | 3 |
| Working life, old jobs and machines | 6 | 2 | 3 |
| Great Lakes and dangerous waters | 4 | 4 | 0 |

The roads and railroad lanes have the strongest proof and the most copycats. Most of the successful channels there upload every day. The Great Lakes row is only four channels, so treat it as a good sign and not as proof.

## The three best options

### Option 1: hidden places of the Great Lakes region (my recommendation)

One strange, dangerous or little-known place per video, across the eight US states and the Canadian province around the Great Lakes: the lakes themselves, their storms and shipwrecks, the land around them, and the engineering built on them.

- **Why:** it joins the two best-measured groups (places and geography at 55%, Great Lakes and ships at 39%). No channel I found covers the whole region across all these subjects.
- **Proof:** Odd Atlas Files (Duluth 97K, Detroit 76K, Milwaukee 59K), Sargasso Films ("America's deadliest lake", 127K), The Salvage Room (Edmund Fitzgerald, 201K), Hidden Canada (Ontario cliffs, 204K), Unreal Earth (Michigan, 139K).
- **Pay:** documentary content for a US and Canadian audience, the best-paying combination in the study.
- **Subscribers:** the better-converting type of channel.
- **Pictures:** US government agencies publish maps, satellite images, photos and accident reports that are free to use. Google Earth Studio gives fly-over shots.
- **Risks:** interest in Great Lakes shipwrecks peaks around 10 November, which has just passed. You need to research a region you may not know. Larger geography channels could move in.

### Option 2: forgotten American roads, bridges, tunnels and railroads

One documented odd story per video about a piece of old infrastructure.

- **Why:** the strongest proof of fast results. Channels two to eight weeks old have videos at 100,000 to 400,000 views.
- **Pay:** an older US audience, likely the best ad rates of the three.
- **Pictures:** the easiest. Old photographs and maps from public archives.
- **Risks:** the most crowded with new channels, most of them uploading daily. Subscribers come slowly, so the 1,000 mark could take 200,000 to 500,000 views.

### Option 3: how work was done, old jobs, tools and machines

Videos such as how loggers felled giant trees before chainsaws, or what a lake freighter crew had to know in the 1970s.

- **Why:** few new channels, and the ones there are doing well (The Forgotten Shift, American Old Times, Trade Archive, Old Glory Iron).
- **Pay:** older US audience.
- **Risks:** the least proof. Its newest success is two weeks old. Subscribers come slowly here too. It needs real knowledge of trades to avoid mistakes that this audience will notice.

## What this research cannot tell you

- **The shares are comparisons, not odds.** I found channels through topic pages, which show videos that already did well. The true success rates are lower than the table suggests, for every niche.
- **I saw views, not watch time or earnings.** A video with many views can still be watched briefly.
- **Channel ages are estimates** from the oldest video listed.
- **It is one day's snapshot.** These lanes are changing week by week, and a lane that is open today can fill in a month.
- **Some samples are small**, and the pay study is one company's 300 channels.
- **Policy risk.** Many of the fast-growing channels upload every day, which is hard to do without heavy automation. YouTube has been removing mass-produced channels from monetization. A channel that publishes every three days with its own research is the safer side of that line.
