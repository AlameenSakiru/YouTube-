# Rules and targets

Checked on 11 November 2026 from third-party guides and news reports. I could not open YouTube's own Help Center, so confirm every number in **YouTube Studio → Earn** before you rely on it.

## What YouTube requires today

| Tier | Subscribers | Plus one of these | What you can earn from |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fan funding | 500 | 3,000 public watch hours in 12 months, or 3M Shorts views in 90 days | Memberships, Super Chat, Shopping |
| Full monetization | 1,000 | 4,000 public watch hours in 12 months, or 10M Shorts views in 90 days | Ads and YouTube Premium revenue |

You also need:

- 2-step verification turned on for your Google account
- No active Community Guidelines strikes
- An AdSense account (you create it during the application)
- To live in a country where the program is available
- Original content that follows the monetization policies

The fan-funding figures (3 uploads in 90 days, 3,000 hours) are from my own knowledge and were not restated in the sources I checked this week.

## The 1 February 2027 deadline

Several outlets report that from **1 February 2027** new applicants need **8,000 watch hours** (or 20M Shorts views) for ad revenue, double today's bar. Channels already in the program keep their long-form ad revenue.

Two things are unclear from the reports:

- **Subscribers:** most sources say the 1,000 figure stays; one does not restate it.
- **Cut-off:** I could not confirm whether you must be *accepted* or only have *applied* before 1 February. Assume you need to be accepted, and apply as early as you can.

From 11 November 2026 you have 82 days. Review can take up to about a month, so aim to **apply by early January**.

## What 4,000 watch hours means in views

4,000 hours is 240,000 minutes of watching.

| Average watch time per view | Views needed |
|---|---|
| 4 minutes | 60,000 |
| 5 minutes | 48,000 |
| 6 minutes | 40,000 |
| 8 minutes | 30,000 |

This is why long-form matters: a 12-minute video that holds people for 5–6 minutes needs far fewer views than a 4-minute video.

Subscribers are often the slower number. As a rough rule of thumb, a new channel gains about 1–2 subscribers per 100 views, so 48,000 views may bring 500–1,000 subscribers. Ask for the subscription in every video.

## What is realistic

- **In 14 days:** four videos live, real data on what works, the first few hundred to few thousand views. A breakout video in this window is possible but not something you can plan on.
- **In 82 days:** about 25 videos at one every three days. One or two videos usually bring most of the views. That is enough attempts to have a fair chance at the threshold.

## What gets a channel rejected

- Buying subscribers, views or watch hours
- Reuploading other people's videos or clips with little change
- Mass-produced videos that all look the same (YouTube calls this inauthentic content)
- Misleading titles and thumbnails that the video does not deliver on

AI tools are allowed. A channel that is only AI voice over stock clips, repeated from a template, risks rejection at review. Write your own scripts, do your own research, and make each video clearly its own work.
