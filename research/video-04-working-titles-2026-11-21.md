## What the data says

1. **"We Drained [water]. Here's What Was Hidden at the Bottom."** is the strongest title of the month: 7 of the top 15 small-channel breakouts. Pearl Harbor got 896,000 views in 5 days on a 1,170-subscriber channel; San Francisco Bay got 152,000 in 5 days on 398 subscribers.
2. **But it only works for the first video about each place.** Lake Michigan has six "We Drained" versions from the last 2 weeks, and the late ones get 110 to 3,142 views. Lake Huron: 557 and 2,564. The winners were first: the Detroit River (120,660 in 6 days), Lake Simcoe (51,266), the St. Lawrence (38,227), the Lake Erie Islands (6,902 in 1 day).
3. **Niagara Falls 1969** (the falls really were dried in 1969): big demand, but done for years by big channels (1.2 million, 670,000, 585,000). Too late.
4. **Lighthouse keepers in winter**: huge this month (731,000 and 1.3 million), but a dozen copies from the last 2 weeks get under 1,500 views, and Stannard Rock has already been covered for 604,000. Too late.
5. **Mackinac is warm right now**: "The Insane Plan to Tunnel Under the Mackinac Bridge" (94,098 in 12 days), "Building the Mackinac Bridge" (10,653 in 1 day), "Why Nobody Wants to Stay at Michigan's Grand Hotel Anymore" (56,647 in 6 days, 2,300 subscribers).
6. **Nobody has made "We Drained the Straits of Mackinac".** Searching for it returns only the crowded Lake Michigan videos.
7. **The Fitzgerald was not a dead topic.** "The Edmund Fitzgerald: From the First Rivet to the Bell" got 77,640 on a 497-subscriber channel, and "Norwegian Sailors Called Lake Superior a POND – Then Detroit Rang a Church Bell 29 Times" got 68,065 on 1,450. Both are statement titles, not questions. Video 3's question title may be part of why it stalled (to confirm with its Studio numbers).

## Decision: video 4

**We Drained the Straits of Mackinac. Here's What Was Hidden at the Bottom.**

- The month's strongest title, word for word, on a place nobody has used it for.
- The place is in demand right now (the Mackinac Bridge videos above), and it is where Lake Michigan and Lake Huron meet.
- Real things at the bottom to show (all to be checked against primary sources before scripting): the Mackinac Bridge's foundations; the Line 5 oil pipelines lying on the lake bed under the bridge, and the 2018 anchor strike that dented them; the shipwrecks of the Straits of Mackinac Shipwreck Preserve, including the Cedarville (1965); and the drowned river valley from thousands of years ago when the lakes were far lower.
- The title is the genre's hypothetical format. The video says in its first minute that nobody can really drain the Straits, and that the "drained" views come from lake-bed survey maps and divers' finds.
