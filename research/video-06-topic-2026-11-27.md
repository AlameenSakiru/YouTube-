## Pick: the SS Henry B. Smith

She left Marquette into the Great Lakes Storm of 1913 and vanished with all 25 men. For 100 years nobody knew where she was. In June 1914 a note in a bottle, supposedly from her, said she broke in two 12 miles east of Marquette, but it was dated 12 November, after she was lost. In May 2013 Ken Merryman, Jerry Eliason and Kraig Smith found her 535 feet down, about 30 miles north of Marquette, broken in two near the middle, her ore cargo spilled; video in June 2013 confirmed her.

Why it can travel far:
- Fits the strongest title pattern of the month ("vanished … found 100 years later").
- Same lake, season, broken hull and hatch-cover question as the Fitzgerald, so video 3's breakout audience is the first audience, and video 3's end screen can point straight at it.
- The deadliest storm in Great Lakes history has proven mass appeal (2.6M on one video).
- A built-in investigation: why did her captain sail when others stayed in port, was the bottle note real, and what does the wreck say.
- No bodies were recovered from her wreck: the story can be told without anything disturbing.

Format: our own investigation (video 3's model) told as a 100-year search: the disappearance, a century of clues and false leads, the hunt, what the wreck shows, the viewer's verdict.

Backup pick: the Carl D. Bradley (1958, Lake Michigan, 2 survivors), the highest proven demand, but heavily covered.

To verify while scripting: the captain's name and decision, the hatch reports at departure, the bottle note's wording and where it was found, the wreck's condition from the 2013 video. Primary reading: Frederick Stonehouse, *Went Missing*; Wikipedia's SS Henry B. Smith and Great Lakes Storm of 1913 pages; MPR News 2013 reports.
