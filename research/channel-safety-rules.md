# Channel safety rules (long-term)

Written 26 November 2026, after the user asked how to avoid YouTube flagging the channel for repetitive or template content. Check every new video against these before scripting.

## What YouTube's rules say (from reporting on the policy; the official page could not be opened from the build machine)

- The YouTube Partner Program's "repetitious content" rule was renamed "inauthentic content" on 15 July 2025 (one unconfirmed source says it was renamed again in 2026 to "Generic or Repetitive Content"). It covers mass-produced or repetitive videos, near-identical videos made from one template, and videos only slightly different from each other.
- It is a monetization rule: breaking it keeps the whole channel out of the Partner Program or removes it. Strikes and channel removal come from the separate spam rules (mass-produced or scraped content, misleading metadata).
- The same intro and outro are allowed if the bulk of each video is different. AI-assisted videos with real value can be monetized; realistic AI content must be disclosed.
- At 1,000 subscribers a human reviewer looks at the channel as a whole.

## Rules for this channel

1. **Vary the format.** No more than two videos in a row in the same format. Videos 4 and 5 are both "We Drained…", and that format is another channel's, so it is retired (see "Unsatisfying or off-putting content" below). Video 6 uses our own format (an investigation like video 3, a single-ship story, a mystery profile).
2. **Write fresh calls to action for each video.** No stock subscribe/like/comment lines copied from video to video; each ask is written for that story.
3. **Every video is its own researched story,** with sources in the description and a fact-check table in its script notes.
4. **Change the look a little each time** (graphics set, colours, title cards), as videos 3, 4 and 5 already do.
5. **Reuse our own images sparingly** (about 10% of shots or less), only where it is genuinely the same ship, place or moment. Never reuse other people's footage or images.
6. **Honest metadata.** Tags only for what is in the video; no copied descriptions; hypothetical titles ("We Drained…") explained in the first minute.
7. **Always tick "Altered or synthetic content: Yes"** while the pictures are AI illustrations, and say so in the description.
8. **Keep the human side visible:** reply to comments, and bring viewers' knowledge into scripts (with permission when naming anyone).
9. **Consider a distinct narrator voice** (a custom voice or the user's own) instead of a widely used stock voice, before the Partner Program application.
10. **Never** work around YouTube's bot checks, buy views or subscribers, or use automated engagement.

## "Unsatisfying or off-putting content" (added 27 November 2026)

The user saw this TeamYouTube reply on X, rejecting another creator's channel from the Partner Program as final:

> We reviewed your channel and found that some of your content isn't in line with our YPP policies for having unsatisfying or off-putting content. [...] Content that might be emotionally manipulative, disturbing, nonsensical, shocking, or otherwise similarly unsatisfying to viewers without delivering significant creative, educational, or other value isn't allowed.

From reporting on the official page (it could not be opened from the build machine), the policy lists:

- emotionally manipulative formulas;
- copying an existing format so closely that videos feel interchangeable;
- shock used mainly to draw views;
- repeatedly using disturbing themes (violence, loss) without building a cohesive story;
- stitching together unrelated or inconsistent AI clips.

It is a monetization rule, judged on the channel as a whole, and the decision can be final.

### Where this channel is exposed, and what we do

| Risk for us | Rule |
|---|---|
| "We Drained… Here's What Was Hidden" is another channel's format (Lie of the Land, Drained Atlas) | Videos 4 and 5 used it; **retire it from video 6 on**. Our own formats only: the investigation (video 3) and others we design ourselves |
| Every video is about disasters and deaths ("loss") | Each video must teach something real (history, science, how and why), with a clear story from start to end and sources in the description. Deaths are told with respect: no remains, no gore, no lingering on suffering |
| Big death-toll numbers and dramatic music could read as manipulative | Use numbers as facts, not as shock. No fake urgency, no guilt in the asks ("it helps a small channel" at most), no "only 1% will watch to the end" style lines |
| AI pictures could look nonsensical | Every picture must match the line it plays under and be historically right (ship type, era, place). Drop or replace any picture with wrong details, garbled text or extra limbs, even if it means a graphic instead |
| Shock thumbnails and titles | The title and thumbnail must be facts the video delivers (like "240 LOST SHIPS", "21 FEET"). No disasters that did not happen, and no "what they found will shock you" |
| Hypothetical premises ("drained") could read as nonsensical | Say early in the video and in the description that it is a thought experiment, and keep it grounded in real surveys and records |

**Before publishing each video, ask:** would a viewer finish it knowing something true they did not know before, and would they feel the title was honest? If either answer is no, fix the video first.
