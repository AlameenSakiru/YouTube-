The results are used in [video-01/upload.md](../video-01/upload.md) and the channel setup in [docs/02-niche-and-channel.md](../docs/02-niche-and-channel.md).

## What YouTube says matters

From YouTube's own help pages:

- **Title, thumbnail and description matter most** for being found. Put one or two main words in both the title and the description, and describe the video in the first lines, which show before "Show more". ([Tips for video descriptions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12948449?hl=en))
- **Tags play a minimal role** unless the subject is often misspelled. Adding lots of tags to a description breaks the spam policy. ([Add tags to your YouTube videos](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/146402?hl=en))
- **Hashtags:** YouTube shows up to three of them beside the title, choosing the ones it considers most engaging. A video with more than 60 hashtags has all of them ignored. ([Find playlists and videos using hashtags](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6390658?hl=en))
- **Misleading metadata is against the rules:** a title, thumbnail, description or tags that promise something the video does not contain. ([Spam, deceptive practices and scams policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801973))

So no set of keywords forces YouTube to push a video. Keywords tell YouTube what the video is about and who to test it on. Whether it is then pushed depends on how those first viewers respond: how many click, and how long they watch.

## How I measured search demand

YouTube's search box suggests phrases as you type, and it suggests the most-searched phrases first. For each phrase I typed it one letter at a time and recorded how early YouTube suggested it. A phrase that appears after only a few letters is searched more than one that needs almost the whole phrase typed.

This gives a ranking, not search counts. Long phrases score slightly better than short ones, so compare phrases of similar length. All 75 measurements are in [keywords-2026-11-12.csv](keywords-2026-11-12.csv).

## What successful small channels put in their metadata

I opened the upload details of 13 videos from small channels in this and similar niches. YouTube blocked automated reading of most other video pages ("Sign in to confirm you're not a bot"), so this sample is small.

| Channel | Video views | Tags | Hashtags | Chapters |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Odd Atlas Files ("Michigan's Upper Peninsula Is More Dangerous Than You Think") | 328,810 | 20 | 7 | 33 |
| DisastersUncovered (Shoreham Airshow) | 325,120 | 17 | 10 | none |
| DisastersUncovered (MV Christena) | 185,605 | 18 | 10 | none |
| Unreal Earth ("Indiana Is Hiding Something Nobody Talks About") | 184,122 | 31 | none | none |
| Chisel Marks (Pennines motorway) | 76,930 | none | 5 | none |
| The Keeper's Logbook (Edmund Fitzgerald) | 53,461 | 21 | 3 | 18 |
| Sargasso Films (Graveyard of the Atlantic) | 40,797 | none | 3 | 14 |

Patterns:

1. **Tags do not decide success.** Chisel Marks and Sargasso Films use no tags at all and still reached 77,000 and 41,000 views.
2. **Channels that do use tags list the exact subject first:** the event, the ship, the place, the people's names, then the wider topic. The Edmund Fitzgerald video starts "Edmund Fitzgerald, SS Edmund Fitzgerald, Edmund Fitzgerald documentary, Edmund Fitzgerald sinking..." and ends "Great Lakes shipwrecks, Great Lakes history".
3. **Hashtags are few and specific:** three to seven, names of the place or event plus one broad one (#GreatLakes, #LakeSuperior, #GreatLakesHistory).
4. **Descriptions open with the story, not "In this video".** For example: "On Monday, the tenth of November, 1975, the freighter Edmund Fitzgerald... was lost on Lake Superior with all twenty-nine men aboard."
5. **Chapters are common** in the longer documentaries (11 to 33).
